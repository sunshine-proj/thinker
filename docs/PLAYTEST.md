# Pupil playtest protocol (ROADMAP task 125)

The automated tests show that every game works and can be won. They cannot show whether pupils enjoy it.
This protocol is for a teacher or researcher running a playtest; it has **not been carried out yet**.

## Who and what

- 6–12 pupils aged about 11–16, in pairs or alone, 30–40 minutes each.
- Consent from pupils and parents or guardians as your school requires. Record no names; use P1, P2, …
- Games: one live game (Moon Landing Live or Projectile Master Live), one classic game from a world they have
  not studied (for example Rolling Race or Entropy Shuffle), and one thought experiment.

## Session

1. Two minutes of introduction: "There are no wrong answers; we are testing the app, not you."
2. Let them choose from the Home page for five minutes. Note what they click first and why.
3. Ask them to play the three activities. Do not help unless they are stuck for more than a minute.
4. Ask them to think aloud. Note where they hesitate, give up, replay or say "again".

## What to record

| Moment | Note |
|---|---|
| First choice on Home | Which card or button, and the reason given |
| Stuck points | Activity, round or level, what they tried, which hint level they opened |
| Quits | Activity and round where they stopped |
| Replays | Activity, and whether they were chasing stars |
| Predictions | Did they predict thoughtfully or type anything to get past? |
| Language | Words or sentences they did not understand (try younger learner mode too) |

## Afterwards

- A three-question survey: What was most fun? What was confusing? Would you open this again at home?
- Turn each repeated problem (seen with two or more pupils) into a task in ROADMAP.md.
